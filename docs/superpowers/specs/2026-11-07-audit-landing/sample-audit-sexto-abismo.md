# Sexto Abismo, a brand audit

## What I looked at

I looked at it the way a customer would. The bottle in someone's hand, the bottle sitting on the shipping boxes, the poster and the mural. Then I typed the website into the browser. It didn't load. I couldn't get to the Instagram either, because the site was going to be my way there.

## What works

I think the mural is the best thing this brand has. Black wall, a teal wall next to it, the cat with four eyes, the man in the big hat, the stairs going nowhere. You stop. You say, hey, what is this? That's the whole job of a wall.

The wordmark is great. The letters change size as they go, so it feels a little drunk and a little strange, and that fits a drink named after an abyss. It still reads from far away.

The box is really good. Black drawings on white, "Hecho en México" on the side. It looks like something you keep, not something you throw away.

## What is in the way

But the website is down. Not slow, not ugly. Gone. Someone sees the mural, likes it, types the name, and gets an error. That's the worst thing that can happen to a brand that spends this much on getting noticed.

On the bottle, the word gin is tiny. From across a bar it reads as a story and a drawing first, and I think most people would guess tequila or mezcal before gin. If I'm a customer, I don't know what you are selling to me.

The story is beautiful, La Felina, El Lacayo, the stranger who wakes up in another world. But it lives on the mural and in the bartender book. The label is too small to carry it, so the person holding the bottle never hears it.

## What I'd change first

Get the website back. One page is enough. Say it's a gin from San Luis Potosí, tell the story in a few lines, show where to buy it, link the Instagram. Then check the domain renewal is on a calendar so this doesn't happen again.

## Does this brand need a studio right now

No. It needs someone to turn the website back on and make the word gin bigger, and that's a week of work, not a studio.
