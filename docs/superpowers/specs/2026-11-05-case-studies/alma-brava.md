## Body (one paragraph = one block)

A mezcal producer from San Luis Potosí came with a brand called Xantolo, named after the Huasteca's Day of the Dead, and asked for a complete renewal. The product was good. The brand read as cheap and themed, the kind of skulls-and-marigolds mezcal that fills a shelf. He wanted something that could compete in a demanding market with a daring presence, without losing the elegance of how mezcal is made.

The first decision was the name. Anything tied to the Day of the Dead or a national holiday would keep the brand inside the costume. The verbal identity had to carry courage, daring and pride, so the name became Alma Brava, Brave Soul: the piece of your soul that fights for what it wants without fear of the limits along the way.

The identity is black, almost nothing else, with a typeface of opulent proportions and a seal. Elegant and fine, so the daring lives in the name and the restraint lives in the object. The brand book's line: "Sentimiento icónico, orgullo de nuestra tierra."

Naming and verbal identity. Logotype, monogram and seal. Brand book. Front and back label and art direction for the bottle. A product photoshoot.

The work was published on Behance and selected by The Dieline and Packaging of the World in 2021. The brand name was registered as a trademark the following year.

## We would say this to a CMO

- If the category wears a costume, take it off before you pick a font.
- A name can carry the daring so the design can stay quiet.

### The problem underneath
- Verbatim brief line (MK+2 case, used in every submission): "The project began with the need to create a brand for a high-quality mezcal that could compete in a demanding market standing out from its competition with a daring and innovative presence, but without losing the elegance that represents the quality and tradition of the processes with which mezcal is created.", Drive MK_BrandCase_AlmaBrava.pdf, 2021-08-24; Gmail 17b08a7817685336, 2021-08-02.
