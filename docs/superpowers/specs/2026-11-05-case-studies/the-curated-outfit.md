## Body (one paragraph = one block)

Rinske Fris styles male founders and executives, one to one, online. Her clients are men whose businesses matured faster than their wardrobes. She had no logo, a website she barely used, and a styling app that accepts only a logo, a few colors and Google fonts. She found Atla through our content: "I feel like your messaging is so strong and I really like your feeling for design."

In her words, the service "solves the disconnect between how someone feels internally and how they show up visually." The market was the problem. Fast content, AI styling apps and generic influencers had filled the space with meaningless advice, so the depth in her work was hard to see. She wanted luxury, but "not Dubai-Loro Piana-luxury." Personal, a bit fun, calm. Her one word for the brand: alignment.

Two moodboards: Structured Ease and Timeless Confidence. She chose the second. Quiet luxury that earns attention instead of demanding it. Refinement, not reinvention. Deep olive over ivory, with gold and brown. A stacked wordmark under the line Intentional Style, a monogram, a family of badges. Miller Display and Miller Text with League Spartan: the kind of serif she had pointed to in the moodboard.

Primary and secondary wordmarks, monogram and favicon, palette, typography, two badge families, photography guidelines, an Instagram profile, post and story system, a brand guidelines book, and a newsletter template built in Flodesk.

Rinske, seeing the old and the new side by side: "now I'm like, what was I doing?" The next morning: "Very happy with the brand direction." Her newsletters have carried the olive wordmark since.

## We would say this to a CMO

- Put the old brand next to the new one. The client's face is the review.
- The tools the client already uses are part of the brief. Design for the app's three fields.
- Luxury is a position, not a look. Ask what kind.

## Facts

### The situation the client came with
- Granola notes, 2025-07-01: virtual styling business; delivers a "Style DNA guide (similar to brand guidelines)", curated wardrobe, outfits via third-party app Nove; 2–3 month engagements; acquisition via DMs; website "underutilized (WordPress/Elementor)"; "No current branding/logo established."
- Rinske, transcript 2025-07-01, on logo: "No. I don't have anything." On naming: "I really like the fact that it's not around me. So when this will explode ... I could somehow sell the business at some point and not have my name on it."
- Why Atla: "I feel like your messaging is so strong and I really like your feeling for design.", transcript 2025-07-01.
- Questionnaire (Notion, 2025-07): "I don't have a logo yet - or at least not one I like or chose on purpose ... Mostly grey/deep emerald green with beige. I like minimalism with a bold edge."
- Constraint: Nove accepts only "my own logo, some colors, and basic Google fonts" (2025-07-01).

### The problem underneath
- Questionnaire: "My service solves the disconnect between how someone feels internally and how they show up visually. Most of my clients are successful men, but their wardrobe hasn't caught up with who they are or where they're going."
- "Thanks to the rise of fast content (TikTok/IG), AI-generated styling apps, and generic influencers the space is bulging with meaningless style advice. This makes it difficult for potential clients to see the depth and strategy in my work."
- Desired perception: "Luxury but not Dubai-Loro Piana-luxury. Niche, but accessible and inviting ... It should feel personal. And a bit fun. Aspiring." Positioning in one word: "alignment". Values: "Empathy, clarity, intentionality, refinement and transformation". Voice: "the friend who tells you what you need to hear ... Not overly authoritative, but definitely calm."
- Audience: "Male founders with mature businesses, executives, and investors. Fathers, of young kids ... ~34–48 years old." "Style has gone on the back burner since having kids and scaling the business."
- Brand as car: "A vintage porsche 911 G serie in dark choco brown."

### The idea chosen and why
- Moodboards 2025-07-11: Direction 01 "Structured Ease" (Clarity, Efficiency, Adaptable) vs Direction 02 "Timeless Confidence" ("Confident, Elevated, Classic"; "quiet luxury that doesn't demand attention, but rather earns it ... It's not about reinvention; it's about refinement").
- Client, email 2025-07-16: "I really like direction 2." Wanted "deeper colors ... than muted/powdery tones", leather "polished" not "rugged/used", a serif "a bit more unique/bold ... like ... The Foundry". "And the monogram idea is really cool too!"
- Friction, client 2025-07-30 on v1: "I feel like my last feedback wasn't taken into account for this round." Fixed in v2.
- Proposal v2, 2025-08-25: "While Larken and Hanken Grotesk are strong typefaces in form, they no longer support the elevated and intentional tone the brand now embodies. Instead, we propose a typographic system built around the Miller family (Display and Text) paired with League Spartan. Miller Display in Semibold ... echoes the refined classicism found in the moodboard's reference (The Foundry)."
- Concept statement (proposals v1/v2, verbatim): "This brand redefines men's personal style with an approach that blends timeless elegance and contemporary edge. Designed for modern men, it champions intentionality by curating elevated, classic pieces that reflect confidence and clarity. The visual language embraces quiet luxury through grounded hues, editorial layouts, and typography that balances tradition with modernity. More than a wardrobe, it's a mindset shift: a refined, structured evolution for men who lead with purpose. By eliminating guesswork and focusing on authenticity, the brand positions itself as a trusted guide, empowering clients to align their outward expression with their inner ambition. It's not reinvention; it's refinement."
