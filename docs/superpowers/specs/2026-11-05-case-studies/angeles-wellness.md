## Body (one paragraph = one block)

Fernando Ramirez started Angeles in his early twenties after months of stomach pain no doctor could explain. Herbal cleansing protocols worked where visits had not. The brand grew fast, burned him out, and lost its customers when he renamed it away from an extreme niche. He came to Atla with a large audience that no longer answered and one ask: the messaging, the branding, the cohesiveness. "It isn't performing well. I know it could be doing way better."

The product is a complete herbal system in two phases, clear then nourish, at a higher price than the shelf. The problem was translation. The site had to teach what the products do so that anyone could follow it. Fernando's words: "Dumb it down." And it could not look cheap, or pushy. Buyers of wellness, the strategy found, are tired of being sold to and tired of fake wellness.

The character we gave the brand is a health strategist: confident, direct, calm, a man in his forties who has seen both sides. The line that carried: your body isn't broken, it's blocked. Three moodboards. Fernando picked the third, the quiet one, with the type and imagery of the first. The logo is Magalie, softened and customized. The gradients follow the sky from sunrise to sunset, one for each product, the change a body goes through on its way back.

Moodboard and strategy deck. Audience validation with three personas. The graphic path: logo, palette in purple, blue and orange, Playfair Display with ITC Avant Garde Gothic, jar boxes, social templates, tote, cards, website hero. Brand guidelines. Packaging: capsules, electrolytes and loose-leaf tea doypacks, labels and box dielines. Event pieces: banner, hat, booth, business cards.

Fernando, at the graphic path presentation: "honestly, I'm impressed right now. I have a smile that I can't hold back." Later, in writing: "I decided to give you all full reins on the rebrand." On the tea packs: "The doypack for the teas look fantastic." The site now opens with the strategy line: "Your body isn't broken. It's backed up."

## We would say this to a CMO

- A big audience that stopped answering is a messaging problem before it is a design problem.
- Give the brand a person. A health strategist writes differently from a healer.
- The line the client repeats back is the one that works. Put it on the homepage.
