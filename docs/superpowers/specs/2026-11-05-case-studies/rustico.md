## Body (one paragraph = one block)

Acre is a resort and farm in San José del Cabo. Its restaurant holds a Michelin Green Star. In the empty center of a circular permaculture garden, the owners were running an open-fire dinner as a pop-up: private buyouts, winemaker dinners, a dark sky above. They called it Rústico. The season was starting and it needed a brand. Cam Watt, the owner, wrote the brief himself: "We want to create an 'experience' or a 'memory'."

His worry was obviousness. "Avoid anything too obvious or predictable. Simple and clear always works for me." Acre already had a brand and a dozen things under it: a bar, the restaurant, weddings, treehouses. Rústico had to read as Acre's, the baby brother of a bigger restaurant to come, without competing with the reputation next door. A place like that cannot out-shout its setting. It has to disappear into it.

We showed four routes. Cam liked the burgundy in one; we recommended the structure of another with that color. He answered: "I trust you." The idea is a circle: seed, harvest, fire, table, community. A wordmark set in Forevs lifts the dinner from casual to ceremonial. A hand-led second logotype keeps the farm in it. Mulled wine, fired brick, terracotta and white linen: firelight in the field, soil underfoot.

Brand strategy and essence, the identity system and a brand book: primary and secondary logotypes, the "by Acre" lockup, color, Maison Neue with Caslon Ionic, a set of stamps, headline frames cut like flame, photography rules and social templates. Logo files and graphic elements, ready for the sign Cam needed that month.

Paolo Vincenti, co-owner: "We consider this approved." Rústico runs under the name, and Acre kept a page of its own for it because, in their words, it is new. The work grew into a brand manual for the whole resort, which the owners said they had been missing for some time.

## We would say this to a CMO

- Next to a star, don't compete. Belong.
- A sub-brand works when it shares the parent's values, not its logo.
- Ask the owner what memory the guest should take home. Design that.
