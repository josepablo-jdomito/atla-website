## Body (one paragraph = one block)

Persona Nutrition has sold personalized vitamin packs for years to a customer in her forties and beyond, in a clinical, evidence-first voice. Jason Brown, its co-founder and CEO, wanted a second brand for women in their twenties and early thirties: non-medical, fun, built around wellness. There was no name he liked, an internal team already drawing looks, and a launch date set for the end of the year. He wanted an outside eye.

The problem was a brand that had never spoken to this customer. Persona's language was cold on purpose. The new one had to sound like a friend who knows her supplements and never makes you feel behind. And it could not look like a pill. Vyv sells daily packs, personalized, morning and night. Every image had to say pack, not capsule.

Jason sent a shortlist. We ranked Vyv first: short, modern, a real brand, with room to grow past vitamins. Of two directions he chose the one we called supplements that embrace you: sunset colors, curves, a happy brand. The logo is lowercase vyv built from one repeated triangle. Read it again and a woman appears in the middle. Expansive radial gradients spread from the center, for vitality.

Naming input and moodboards. Logo, logotype and a 3D mark. Brand guidelines with photography rules and Instagram formats. The gradient system as editable files. Packaging: the shipping box and large and small dispenser boxes, in lilac and maroon, print-ready. The onboarding questionnaire. The homepage, desktop and mobile, handed to their developers.

Jason, after the guidelines: "I think it's amazing, keeping it both feminine, but also a strong color palette. I love the way the logo has developed." And later: "complete different energy than I would've ever expected for this brand." Vyv launched in January 2026. The next project was Persona itself.

## We would say this to a CMO

- A sub-brand for a new customer needs a new voice before it needs a new logo.
- Pick the name that can outgrow the category.
- Show the product the way it is sold. A single pill is a lie for a pack.

## Facts

### The situation the client was in
- Persona Nutrition: founded 1999 as Vitamin Packs, "built it twice and sold it twice", sold to Nestlé Health Science 2019, bought back 2024; ~22 people; Persona customer is 35+, AOV $138, Intro transcript, 2025-10-03; Bios docx, 2025-10-22.
- Jason: "we're gonna build a new brand under our brand. So a sub brand… it's gonna service women between the ages of 20 and 35 predominantly, 24 to 34… nonmedical, much more fun and whimsical, and around wellness." "We wanna duplicate what Care Of did." Launch "in November.", Intro transcript, 2025-10-03.
- Expected economics: "AOV about 80 and a LTV of maybe six", Intro, 2025-10-03.
- No name, no brand: working name "MyVitaPaks" ("we don't like it"); internal UX/UI team already making looks; "we wanted to go and find somebody outside of us who can provide fresh perspective.", Intro, 2025-10-03.
- Jason, 2025-10-15 email to JP: "I am sitting on pins and needles trying to not be me…. Anything you want to show me or tell me before Friday????"

### The problem underneath
- A legacy clinical brand trying to reach a customer it has never spoken to: Persona "has more of the science backed medical evidence cold language. With this one, [we] wanna go more… like someone friendly chatting with you about what pills you should take." (Cavan, Intro, 2025-10-03).
- Trust gap: "our trust pilot reviews are awesome at Persona with over 10K of them yet vyv has zero out of the gate.", Jason email, 2025-10-29.
- "We've done 25 questionnaires with different looks and different feels. And none of them… have been remarkable." The last page "needs your attention… I've emotionally taken this questionnaire… and I now emotionally need [to] convert.", Jason, Brand Guide review transcript, 2025-10-22.
- Must not look like a single-pill supplement: "we are in the convenience pack business not supplements as stand alone pills… we PERSONALIZE each and every pack with over 5 Trillion Combinations.", Jason email, 2025-10-29. Kendra, 2025-10-31: hero showed "a hand holding a single pill. We don't sell single pills, but daily vitamin packs."

### The idea chosen and why
- Naming (2025-10-08 email): Jason shortlisted Vyv / Myvitapacks / Kit / Vivva; JP ranked Vyv first: "Short, modern, and feels like a real brand. The spelling is intriguing and has room to grow beyond just vitamins… It's my clear favorite." Name confirmed Vyv, vyvnutrition.com (call 2025-10-09).
- Direction 1 chosen over Direction 2 on 2025-10-09: "supplements or vitamins that embrace you… inspired by the colors of the sunset… a happy brand. A brand that embraces you"; curves "to transmit this friendly communication." Palette: warm oranges, browns, burgundy (#883939), purple accent; photography natural, outdoors, product as protagonist. Jason: "It feels very feminine, very bold, very clear." Cavan: a "great evolution" from Persona's medical tone., Granola notes + transcript, 2025-10-09.
- JP's Persona-connection argument: "there is no harm in having persona there. It's not the bad uncle"; Persona "has a couple $300,000,000 behind it" (Jason). Jason moved from total separation to "persona would not be hidden. It would be acknowledged.", 2025-10-09.
- Logo (2025-10-17): lowercase "vyv" built from a repeated triangle, feminine curves, works as logotype and symbol; hidden readings: "w" / woman, "mujer" in the center, a high heel, mountains and a wave in the y. Expansive radial gradients "from the center… symbolizing this expansion and vitality." Jason: "I love the direction… I think you've done a fucking amazing job." Cavan's readability concern ("byp" vs "w") solved by "Vyv Nutrition" in adjacent copy.
- Brand guide (2025-10-21/22): tagline dropped from the logo top; "come up with three max three buy lines that we use in different places… to emote different emotions"; palette shifted "Brown less… purple more" (Jason).
- Typography: Chillax (Fontshare) for the system; logo is a customized font, Mariela email, 2025-10-22; VYV Informe.txt.
