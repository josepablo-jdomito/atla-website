## Full descriptions, as they will be stored

### 1. Alma Brava (`alma-brava`)

Alma Brava is a mezcal from San Luis Potosí that began life as Xantolo. Its producer came to Atla for a complete renewal: a name, an identity and a seal.

The product was good. The old brand dressed the mezcal in the Day of the Dead the way a movie would. He wanted something that could compete in a demanding market with a daring presence, and keep the elegance of how mezcal is made.

### 2. Ando (`ando`)

Ando is a berry-flavored gummy from Brooklyn made to prevent and ease alcohol flush. In 2023, before the first production run, its founders came to Atla for a brand.

Peter Czepiga and Ray Margono met as college freshmen. They wanted a brand that made health feel joyful.

### 3. Angeles Wellness (`angeles-wellness`)

Angeles Wellness is an herbal cleansing brand from Los Angeles, started by Fernando Ramirez. In 2025 he came to Atla for the messaging, the branding and the cohesiveness of the brand.

He started Angeles after herbal cleansing worked for him. The brand had grown fast under another name and needed to speak to its audience again.

### 4. Anything AI (`anything-ai`) · HOLD

Anything, first called Create, is an AI app builder: you type what you want and it comes back as a working product. The company came to Atla with a launch date on the calendar and a brand to rebuild before it.

The company had been underestimated, and bigger rivals took the attention.

### 5. Arc Studio (`arc-studio`) · HOLD

Arc Studio was planned as a boutique Pilates and recovery studio in Abu Dhabi, built around calm. Its founders came to Atla before opening, with no identity yet.

The founders were opening infrared-heated Pilates, sculpt, barre and mat classes, with saunas and cold plunges. The interior was decided, oak and curves and green, and the name was chosen, Arc, for movement, growth and story.

### 6. Baristio (`baristio`) · HOLD

Baristio is a coffee company in the making, built around the person behind the bar. Emilio Orozco came to Atla in 2025, before anything had launched.

Its app lets you choose your barista before you choose your drink, and the business planned to start on delivery apps, with a shop to follow. The contract put the goal in one line: a solid brand from day one.

### 7. Bond & Bloom (`bond-and-bloom`) · HOLD

Bond & Bloom teaches parents of babies and toddlers to read their child. Paola Díaz, a developmental specialist, came to Atla with no name and no logo.

She wanted to reach parents on Instagram first, then through a free guide, then courses, then sessions one to one. Her challenge, in her words, was to stand out "in a fast, noisy, and trend-driven market" while staying calm inside it.

### 8. Bovi Health (`bovi-health`)

Bovi Health sells grass-fed beef colostrum and beef organ capsules, made in the USA. Its founders came to Atla at the end of 2024 for the brand, the packaging and a site to sell from.

Shannon Berg, a former college athlete, and Colson Myers, who has trained all his life, say health should be simple. Their products were made by a contract manufacturer.

### 9. Casa Colora (`casa-colora`)

Casa Colora was planned as a creative space for children and families in Monterrey. Its founders came to Atla in 2025 for the name and the brand.

### 10. Castro Capital (`castro-capital`)

Castro Capital invests in real estate development in San Luis Potosí. In 2021 its co-founders came to Atla for an identity to match their ambition.

Moisés and Sebastián Castro Martín del Campo wanted the firm to be an ally to clients building commercial property, someone to help them make the best choices along the way.

### 11. Hanks Leather (`hanks-leather`)

Hanks Leather is a heritage leather house from Upstate New York. In 2025 its director, Mia Calleo, came to Atla for a new identity and a website.

### 12. Huémac (`huemac`)

Huémac, from Querétaro, set out to be a platform for outdoor experiences, booking adventures with trusted guides. In 2021 it came to Atla for a strategy and an identity.

Its audience ran from the young and adventurous to families and to travellers who wanted the softest version of the outdoors, with safety and trust built in.

### 13. Mundã (`munda`)

Mundã is a project of vacation and long-stay homes in La Saladita, on the coast of Guerrero. Atla made its brand in 2022, to sell a way of living close to the waves and the neighbors.

Its full name is Mundã, Aldea del Mar, a village by the sea. The brand had to work on posters, on paper and on the wall at the entrance.

### 14. Oxylife (`oxylife`)

Oxylife was oxygenated water in a can, produced in Prague for the United Arab Emirates. In 2021 Suhail Alameri came to Atla for the brand and the can label, before the name was settled.

He described it as the most stable oxygenated water in the world, sealed in a patented can coated on the inside.

### 15. Pathize Health (`pathize-health`)

Pathize Health built an app for people living with Long COVID, ME/CFS and POTS. In 2023 the team came to Atla on a referral, with a website relaunch close behind.

The app reads data from wearables like Apple Health, Fitbit, Garmin and Polar, and helps each person pace the day against a personal limit. The referral came from the founder of Peachy.

### 16. Pax & Beneficia (`pax-and-beneficia`)

Pax & Beneficia is a coffee roaster and café group from Dallas-Fort Worth, opened in Las Colinas in 2019. The company came to Atla to bring its brand up to the mission.

The name means peace and blessings, and the cafés are built on it: a guest is an honored visitor. The company sells roasted coffee and handcrafted syrups online as well as over the counter.

### 17. Peachy Patients (`peachy-patients`)

Peachy was a healthcare startup built for patients. In 2022 the team came to Atla after trying to brand the company themselves.

Its founder, Lex Oiler, is a designer who became a founder. Lex put the problem in one sentence: "I had no idea branding for my startup would end up being the hardest part of my job."

### 18. Persona (`persona`)

Persona Nutrition builds a vitamin plan for each customer. The company came to Atla for a rebrand built around one idea: only one you.

### 19. PuppyPy (`puppypy`)

PuppyPy, from Los Angeles, makes silicone holders that clip a tracking tag onto a dog's collar. In 2022 its founder, Gilad Rom, came to Atla to rename and rebuild the company, then called Huan.

The product had already proved itself. Atla took it on that spring, as it began working with clients in the United States.

### 20. Reggie (`reggie`) · HOLD

Reggie is an American brand of calming chews, supplements and feeder mats for dogs. In 2025 its new owners rebuilt the brand with Atla, through the studio Vapor, while moving from direct sales into retail.

The name is short for regimen. The owners, from New Zealand, inherited a brand caught between its original look and a refresh from the year before. A trade show was on the calendar.

### 21. Rústico (`rustico`)

Rústico is an open-fire dinner at Acre, a resort and farm in San José del Cabo. With the season starting, its owner wrote Atla the brief himself.

Acre's restaurant holds a Michelin Green Star. In the empty center of a circular permaculture garden, the owners were running the dinner as a pop-up, planned for private buyouts and winemaker dinners, a dark sky above. Cam Watt's brief: "We want to create an 'experience' or a 'memory'."

### 22. Sexto Abismo (`sexto-abismo`) · HOLD

Sexto Abismo is a gin from San Luis Potosí, named after the Sótano de las Golondrinas, one of the largest abysses on earth. José Pablo started it in 2021 with partners from San Luis Potosí and built its brand himself.

There was no outside client and no brief, only a bottle to fill and a region to put on the label.

### 23. Shop Latinx (`shop-latinx`)

Shop Latinx is a marketplace for Latino brands in beauty, fashion and lifestyle, from Santa Monica. In 2022 its founder, Brittany Chavez, came to Atla to rebrand it without falling into stereotypes.

She built it to give those brands visibility in the North American market. It started as a fashion brand and, as Atla wrote at the time, it lacked the folklore of the culture it served.

### 24. Tequila Unido (`tequila-unido`) · HOLD

Tequila Unido is a tequila built alone by Carlos Alvarado, a Texan with roots in Jalisco. He came to Atla with a symbol, a bottle and a blue label; the brand had to be clarified and kept.

Carlos calls himself "tejano jalisciense." He registered the name, partnered with a family distillery in Arandas founded in 1915, and carried samples by bus to Dallas distributors.

### 25. The Branding Academy (`the-brand-academy`)

The Branding Academy is José Pablo's weekly newsletter for founders and the people who run brands. It was named at the end of 2025 and runs on Substack; its identity treats it as a school.

It began as a reply on Instagram: comment academy, and he sends you a branding strategy every week.

### 26. The Curated Outfit (`the-curated-outfit`)

The Curated Outfit is a personal styling service for men who run companies. In 2025 its founder, Rinske Fris, came to Atla for a first brand, luxurious but personal.

### 27. Internet Empires (`the-internet-empires`)

Internet Empires is a media brand for internet entrepreneurs, creators and online builders, from Los Angeles. In 2025 its founder, Ish Verduzco, came to Atla to rebrand it.

It reaches people building internet businesses through social media, email and in-person events. Its original mark was a globe. The new identity keeps the globe with a crown, sets the name in Blinker, and comes with photography of the community and templates for flyers, posts and quote cards. Ish, on delivery: "Love how it all came out."

### 28. TigreTigre (`tigretigre`)

TigreTigre is a sushi and sake restaurant on Avenida Chapultepec in San Luis Potosí. José Pablo and his partners opened it in 2023, and the brand was made before the doors opened.

The pitch fits on a sticker: after-office sake and food. Japanese comfort plates made with good ingredients, cocktails and premium sake, a place to land when the day ends.

### 29. Torre Avancer (`torre-avancer`)

Torre Avancer is an office tower on Avenida Chapultepec in San Luis Potosí, one of Grupo Avancer's holdings. The tower was already recognised when the owners asked Atla for its identity; the building came before the brand.

Grupo Avancer has businesses in flavors and fragrances, finance, real estate and hotels. The tower's irregular geometry was drawn to recall the semi-desert around the city.

### 30. Vyv (`vyv`)

Vyv is a vitamin brand for younger women from Persona Nutrition. In 2025 Persona came to Atla to name it, draw it and pack it before its first order.
