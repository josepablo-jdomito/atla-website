## Full descriptions, as they will be stored

### 1. Alma Brava (`alma-brava`)

Alma Brava is a mezcal from San Luis Potosí, renewed from a brand called Xantolo. Its producer came to Atla for a complete renewal: a name, an identity and a seal.

The product was good. The brand looked childish and cheap, dressed in the Day of the Dead the way a movie would dress it. He wanted something that could compete in a demanding market with a daring presence, and keep the elegance of how mezcal is made.

### 2. Ando (`ando`)

Ando is a berry-flavored gummy from Brooklyn made to prevent and ease alcohol flush. In 2023, before the first production run, its founders came to Atla for a brand.

Peter Czepiga and Ray Margono met as college freshmen. Ray is Indonesian and had lived with the flush, also known as Asian flush, his whole adult life. They wanted a brand that made health feel joyful.

### 3. Angeles Wellness (`angeles-wellness`)

Angeles Wellness is an herbal cleansing brand from Los Angeles, started by Fernando Ramirez. He came to Atla with a large audience that no longer answered.

Fernando started Angeles after a long stretch of stomach pain no doctor could explain. Herbal cleansing protocols worked where the visits had not. The brand grew fast, burned him out, and lost its customers when he renamed it away from an extreme niche. His one ask: "It isn't performing well. I know it could be doing way better."

### 4. Anything AI (`anything-ai`)

Anything is an AI app builder, launched as Create: you type what you want and it comes back as a working product. The company came to Atla with a launch date on the calendar and a brand to rebuild before it.

The company had been underestimated. Some early users had a bad experience, and bigger rivals took the attention.

### 5. Arc Studio (`arc-studio`)

Arc Studio is a boutique Pilates and recovery studio in Abu Dhabi, built around calm. Its founders came to Atla before opening, with a name and no identity.

Two partners were opening infrared-heated Pilates, sculpt, barre and mat classes, with saunas and cold plunges. The interior was decided, oak and curves and green, and the name was chosen, Arc, for movement, growth and story. The studio would be the first in the city to offer heated sculpt and mat Pilates.

### 6. Baristio (`baristio`)

Baristio is a coffee company built around the person behind the bar. Emilio Orozco, a co-founder, came to Atla in 2025, before anything had launched.

Its app lets you choose your barista before you choose your drink, and the business planned to start on delivery apps, with a shop to follow. The contract put the goal in one line: a solid brand from day one.

### 7. Bond&Bloom (`bond-and-bloom`)

Bond & Bloom teaches parents of babies and toddlers to read their child, from Austin. Paola Díaz, a developmental specialist, came to Atla with no name, no logo and no founding story she believed in.

She wanted to reach parents on Instagram first, then through a free guide, then courses, then sessions one to one. Her challenge, in her words, was to stand out "in a fast, noisy, and trend-driven market" while staying calm inside it.

### 8. Bovi Health (`bovi-health`)

Bovi Health sells grass-fed beef colostrum and beef organ capsules, from Charlotte. Its founders came to Atla at the end of 2024 for the brand, the packaging and a site to sell from.

Shannon Berg, a former college athlete, and Colson Myers, who has trained all his life, say health should be simple. Their products were made in the USA by a contract manufacturer.

### 9. Casa Colora (`casa-colora`)

Casa Colora is a creative space for children and families in Monterrey. Two industrial designers came to Atla for a name and a brand before the space existed.

### 10. Castro Capital (`castro-capital`)

Castro Capital invests in real estate development in San Luis Potosí. In 2021 its co-founders came to Atla for an identity to match their ambition.

Moisés and Sebastián Castro Martín del Campo wanted the firm to be an ally to clients building commercial property, someone to help them make the best choices along the way.

### 11. Hanks Leather (`hanks-leather`)

Hanks Leather is a heritage leather house from Upstate New York, making belts since 1949. The company came to Atla to be known for women's accessories too, without losing the men.

### 12. Huémac (`huemac`)

Huémac is a platform for outdoor experiences from Querétaro, booking adventures with trusted guides. In 2021 it came to Atla for a strategy and an identity.

Its audience ran from the young and adventurous to families and to travellers who wanted the softest version of the outdoors, with safety and trust built in.

### 13. Mundã (`munda`)

Mundã is a village of vacation and long-stay homes in La Saladita, on the coast of Guerrero. Atla made its brand in 2022, to sell a way of living close to the waves and the neighbors.

Its full name is Mundã, Aldea del Mar, a village by the sea. The brand had to work on posters, on paper and on the wall at the entrance.

### 14. Oxylife (`oxylife`)

Oxylife is oxygenated water in a can, produced in Prague and sold in the United Arab Emirates. Suhail Alameri brought it to Atla in 2021, before the brand had a settled name.

He described it as the most stable oxygenated water in the world, sealed in a patented can coated on the inside.

### 15. Pathize Health (`pathize-health`)

Pathize Health builds an app for people living with Long COVID, ME/CFS and POTS, in Los Angeles. In 2023 the team came to Atla on a referral, with a website relaunch close behind.

The app reads data from wearables like Apple Health, Fitbit, Garmin and Polar, and helps each person pace the day against a personal limit. The referral came from the founder of Peachy.

### 16. Pax & Beneficia (`pax-and-beneficia`)

Pax & Beneficia is a coffee roaster and café group from Dallas-Fort Worth, opened in Las Colinas in 2019. A brand existed and had fallen behind the mission; the company came to Atla to catch it up.

The name means peace and blessings, and the cafés are built on it: a guest is an honored visitor. The company sells roasted coffee and handcrafted syrups online as well as over the counter.

### 17. Peachy Patients (`peachy-patients`)

Peachy is a healthcare startup built for patients, from Los Angeles. In 2022 the team came to Atla after trying to brand the company themselves.

Its founder, Lex Oiler, is a designer who became a founder. Lex put the problem in one sentence: "I had no idea branding for my startup would end up being the hardest part of my job."

### 18. Persona (`persona`)

Persona Nutrition is the personalized vitamin pioneer, with daily packs that carry each customer's name. The company came to Atla for a rebrand built around one idea: only one you.

### 19. PuppyPy (`puppypy`)

PuppyPy makes silicone holders that clip a tracking tag onto a dog's collar, from Los Angeles. In 2022 its founder, Gilad Rom, came to Atla to rename and rebuild the company, then called Huan.

The product had already proved itself. We took it on that spring, as the studio was starting to work with clients in the United States.

### 20. Reggie (`reggie`)

Reggie makes calming chews, supplements and feeder mats for dogs, from San Diego. New owners came to Atla in 2025 to rebuild the brand while moving from direct sales into retail.

The name is short for regimen. The owners, from New Zealand, inherited the original brand, a refresh from the year before, and the watered-down version the old team had actually shipped, white jars and all. A trade show was on the calendar.

### 21. Rústico (`rustico`)

Rústico is an open-fire dinner at Acre, a resort and farm in San José del Cabo. The season was starting and it needed a brand; the owner wrote the brief himself.

Acre's restaurant holds a Michelin Green Star. In the empty center of a circular permaculture garden, the owners were running the dinner as a pop-up: private buyouts, winemaker dinners, a dark sky above. Cam Watt's brief: "We want to create an 'experience' or a 'memory'."

### 22. Sexto Abismo (`sexto-abismo`)

Sexto Abismo is a gin from San Luis Potosí, named after one of the deepest abysses on earth. José Pablo started it in 2021 with Oscar Gómez and Rodrigo Espinosa, and branded it at his studio before Atla.

The name comes from the Sótano de las Golondrinas in the Huasteca Potosina. There was no outside client and no brief, only a bottle to fill and a region to put on the label.

### 23. Shop Latinx (`shop-latinx`)

Shop Latinx is a marketplace for Latino brands in beauty, fashion and lifestyle, from Santa Monica. In 2022 its founder, Brittany Chavez, came to Atla to rebrand it without falling into stereotypes.

She built it to give those brands visibility in the North American market. It started as a fashion brand and, as we wrote at the time, it lacked the folklore of the culture it served.

### 24. Tequila Unido (`tequila-unido`)

Tequila Unido is a tequila built alone by Carlos Alvarado, a Texan with roots in Jalisco. He came to Atla with a symbol, a bottle and a blue label; the brand had to be clarified and kept.

Born in Eagle Pass, with grandparents from Guadalajara and a father who sang mariachi, he calls himself "tejano jalisciense." He registered the name, partnered with a family distillery in Arandas founded in 1915, and carried samples by bus to Dallas distributors.

### 25. The Brand Academy (`the-brand-academy`)

The Branding Academy is José Pablo's weekly newsletter for founders and the people who run brands. It was named at the end of 2025 and runs on Substack; Atla gave it the identity of a school.

It began as a reply on Instagram: comment academy, and he sends you a branding strategy every week. A seal, and a row of bound books in pastel cloth.

### 26. The Curated Outfit (`the-curated-outfit`)

The Curated Outfit styles male founders and executives, run from Amsterdam by Rinske Fris. She came to Atla with no logo and a website she barely used, asking for quiet luxury.

### 27. The Internet Empires (`the-internet-empires`)

Internet Empires is a media brand for internet entrepreneurs, creators and online builders, from Los Angeles. In 2025 its founder, Ish Verduzco, came to Atla to rebrand it.

It reaches people building internet businesses through social media, email and in-person events. Its original mark was a globe. The new identity keeps the globe with a crown, sets the name in Blinker, and comes with photography of the community and templates for flyers, posts and quote cards. Ish, on delivery: "Love how it all came out."

### 28. TigreTigre (`tigretigre`)

TigreTigre is a sushi and sake restaurant on Avenida Chapultepec in San Luis Potosí. José Pablo and his partners opened it in 2023, and the brand was made before the doors opened.

The pitch fits on a sticker: after-office sake and food. Japanese comfort plates made with good ingredients, cocktails and premium sake, a place to land when the day ends.

### 29. Torre Avancer (`torre-avancer`)

Torre Avancer is an office tower on Avenida Chapultepec in San Luis Potosí, one of Grupo Avancer's holdings. The building came before the brand; it had already won awards when the owners asked Atla for its identity.

Grupo Avancer has businesses in flavors and fragrances, finance, real estate and hotels. The tower's irregular geometry was drawn to recall the semi-desert around the city.

### 30. Vyv (`vyv`)

Vyv is a vitamin brand for younger women from Persona Nutrition, in Bellevue, Washington. Jason Brown, Persona's CEO, came to Atla with no name he liked and a launch date set for the end of the year.
