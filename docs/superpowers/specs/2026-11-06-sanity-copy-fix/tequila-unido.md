## description (as it will be stored)

<!-- description:start -->
Carlos Alvarado built Tequila Unido alone. Born in Eagle Pass, Texas, with grandparents from Guadalajara and a father who sang mariachi, he calls himself "tejano jalisciense." He registered the name, partnered with a family distillery in Arandas founded in 1915, carried samples by bus to Dallas distributors, and came to us with a symbol, a bottle and a blue label. The brand had to be clarified and kept.

Look at a tequila shelf and the blue is everywhere. His symbol, a rigid geometric knot in a circle, read like a keyboard key. And Carlos set conditions. His culture could never become a costume, and the story could not be about him: "hay que hacer mi historia en la historia de todos." Unido had to mean something you could say at a bar.

The idea was already in his brief: "unido is how you order it." Good tequila is offered and shared. We took the mariachi, his father's trade, as the cultural key without naming the family: a medallion label shaped by the sombrero, ornaments from the brooches of a mariachi jacket, a symbol that reads as rope, ribbon or bow. Paper, deep red, gold and green. The eagle was redrawn: the first one, in his words, was a pigeon.

A moodboard and the brand direction. Labels for Blanco, Reposado, Añejo and Extra Añejo, and the small bottle. Brand guidelines with the custom wordmark, symbol, badges, Schoolbook and Latino Gothic, photography direction and the Instagram system. A coming-soon page. The case box and the shipper art, ready to print for the distributor.

Carlos, on the first presentation: "sí me gusta mucho el concepto del sombrero del mariachi con el logo, siento que sí me entendieron." On the final white Blanco label: "I love it. I think that's the one." And: "I feel extremely blessed and happy to have you guys on board."
