## description (as it will be stored)

<!-- description:start -->
Paola Díaz is a developmental specialist. She wanted to teach parents of babies and toddlers to read their child: on Instagram first, then through a free guide, then courses, then sessions one to one. She had no name, no logo and, by her own account, no founding story she believed in. Her challenge, in her words, was to stand out "in a fast, noisy, and trend-driven market" while staying calm inside it.

Content about early development swings between clinical advice nobody can apply at home and trendy posts that are easy to consume and leave nothing behind. Parents end up confused and tired. The opening was a calm, bilingual voice that puts connection first, for the earliest years of a child's life. Underneath it sits one belief: understanding comes before doing, and a regulated parent raises a regulated child.

We showed her Gentle Bloom, warm and drawn by hand, and Curious Guidance, with crayon strokes and vivid accents. Paola chose the first and asked to bring in the crayon marks from the second, with less orange and a softer logo. The ampersand in the name became the symbol, a parent holding a child. Fraunces brings the warmth and GT Walsheim the clarity. The photography shows parenting as it is, close and imperfect.

The name. A strategy deck with the belief, point of view, values, audience, positioning, messaging and tagline options. Brand guidelines with the wordmark, symbol, palette, type, illustrations for each stage of a baby's growth, the Instagram system, the newsletter and sets for recording. Website mockups. Then a copywriting system with content pillars, series and a launch calendar, and a run of Instagram carousels written to it.

At the presentation Paola said: "me encantó cómo explicas la marca, o sea, sí es más o menos todo lo que tenía en mente." The brand is live on Instagram and in her newsletter, set in Fraunces on soft linen.
