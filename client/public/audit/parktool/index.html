06Already here

Park Tool already has what a proud brand is built on.

The blue

A color with a story.

The tools were red when Park Tool made them for Schwinn dealers. When Park Tool began selling to every shop, it switched to blue, and the blue is now a registered trademark.

The standard

The brand the industry trusts.

In 2015, Eric Hawkins said Shimano, SRAM and Race Face all bring their new parts to Park Tool, and described the company as “a Switzerland.”

The teacher

Where riders learn.

Calvin Jones led education at Park Tool for 28 years and taught a generation of riders. The Big Blue Book is in its fourth edition, alongside the Repair Help library and 158 million YouTube views.

The origin

A stand from a St. Paul shop.

Howard Hawkins and Art Engstrom built a repair stand in the back of their St. Paul bike shop. Park Tool was founded in 1963, and Eric Hawkins, Howard’s son, runs it today.

08What’s at stake

What waiting costs.

The threats and potential losses if Park Tool doesn’t invest in the next era of its brand.

  1. 01

    A competitor rebrands and takes your place.

    A rival with a sharper brand can launch tomorrow and claim the spot you thought was yours. Markets remember the brand they can describe in one sentence, and it doesn’t have to be the one with the better product.

  2. 02

    You end up competing on price.

    When people can’t tell brands apart, they compare features and price. Without a clear position, every discount becomes your argument.

  3. 03

    Your fans can’t explain you.

    People recommend what they can put into words. If your best customers can’t say what you stand for, word of mouth stays small.

  4. 04

    Someone else names your category.

    If you don’t say what you stand for, a competitor will say it for you, and you’ll spend years correcting their version.
